Intelligence

AI at every step of your search

Something in Jobadabra is reading, suggesting or checking at every step. You decide each one; it never presses Submit. Below, “Claude” is whichever AI you picked: Free AI, or Claude or OpenAI with your own key or plan.

  1. FindIt reads boards and careers pages
  2. PrepareYour CV and letter
  3. ApplyIt fills the form. It never presses Submit
  4. TrackReplies
  5. LearnEvery interview and rejection teaches the next application.
  6. TrustYour AI

Every card: what it does, what Claude sees, what it never sees. Open “How it works” for the detail.

FIND

Finds what is worth your time

It reads boards and careers pages, then tells you which jobs fit, and why.

Job boards of your country

Names the boards people really use for your kind of work.

  • countries, places, role words
  • your CV
How it works

Claude names the job boards people really use in your countries for your kind of work (a general board, a developer or hospitality board), with their public search page; Jobadabra reads those pages with your own search words, like any careers page. A board that refuses automated reading is offered for you to open; one that never has jobs is dropped. Claude sees your countries, places and role words, never your CV.

A scout that keeps learning

Looks for employers and career pages, and learns from each probe.

  • place words, page text of a careers page
  • your CV
How it works

Claude proposes employers, their own job sites and company lists to look at, and with Claude Code searches the web for a job site no address leads to, then learns from what each probe found. It starts from the companies of the countries your places are in (Claude says which country each place word is in), and Claude says, in any language, whether a careers page has open jobs, none right now, or is no careers page at all; that answer is kept for the page. The shared employer list grows with every install.

Reads any job site

Works out a job list it has never seen, once, then remembers it.

  • an outline of the page
  • anything about you
How it works

When the extension's quick guess cannot read a job list you opened, Claude reads an outline of the page (its repeated blocks and paging buttons, a few samples, never anything about you) and says which blocks are jobs, which line is the title, employer and place, and how to reach the next page. That recipe is kept for the site, so the next visit needs no AI; Claude is asked again only when the site changes. A fast model reads first; when it finds no list, a stronger one tries that page once.

Roles that fit you

Suggests neighbouring roles: a photographer’s studio assistant, a seller’s cashier.

  • your Profile, search words
  • the jobs
How it works

On Strategy, Claude reads your Profile and your role words and suggests up to eight neighbouring roles you could do (a photographer: studio assistant, retoucher; a shop seller: cashier, visual merchandiser), each with the word to search for and how many open jobs it would add in your places. One click adds it; "Not now" sets them aside for good. At most once a day; Claude sees your Profile and search words, never the jobs.

Knows where each job is

Says if a job is in your places and whether you may work there.

  • location names, your places, work rights
  • your CV
How it works

Your places are read by Claude, not matched as words: each new job location ("1211 Genf", "Biel/Bienne", "Wallisellen", "Remote - EMEA") is shown to Claude with your places as you wrote them (best places, anywhere in, abroad, remote or not), and it says whether the job is in one of your best places, in your places, or outside them, and whether your work rights as you wrote them (a permit, a citizenship, "EU") let you work there without a visa. When a location names no place of its own (only "Switzerland", or nothing), Claude reads the start of that posting to find where the job is ("at the Geneva site"), on any site; until then the job is neither scored nor closed. Each location and each such posting is decided once; changing your places or work rights asks again. Claude sees location names, your place words and your work rights, and for those postings their title and text, never your CV.

Sorts titles your words miss

A “Client Advisor” can be a shop seller: it judges, in any language.

  • job titles, your search words
  • anything else
How it works

Only the place is strict. A job in your places whose title your role words miss ("Client Advisor", "Collaborateur Magasin" for a shop seller) is shown to Claude with your search words; it says which titles could be your kind of work, in any language. Each title is decided once, then the fit score grades the job as usual. Claude sees only titles and your search words.

Fit score out of 100

Role, place, pay, growth and risk, with the reasons.

  • your Profile, the posting
How it works

Role, place, pay, growth and risk scored against your Profile, with the reasons. Re-scored when your Profile changes. Paused, and said so, while your Profile is still the empty template.

An answer for every empty field

Proposes what each empty box needs, from your details or CV.

  • the field’s label, your details
  • consents, demographics, facts only you know
How it works

When a form is left with empty fields, the Applying page shows each one with its proposed answer: what the fill tried and the form did not take, or the detail it asks for, from your details or your CV. Claude reads which detail a label asks for ("Rue et numéro" is your street, "Formule d'appel" your salutation), in any language, from the label alone, once per label. For a menu, Claude picks the form's own choice that means the same ("Monsieur" on a Madam/Sir menu is Sir), never a word the form doesn't offer. Use fills it in the form and keeps it for future forms; you can change it first. When nothing you gave states it, Claude types your most likely answer, when it is fairly sure, from your Profile and what the posting expects (a salary, years with a tool, a yes/no screening question), and you check every answer before you submit. A low-confidence guess, or a guess at a contact detail, a link, a knockout question (work permit, sponsorship, location) or a choice such as SMS notifications is only proposed, for you to confirm; never anything for consents, demographic questions or facts only you know.

The job list, not the home page

Looks for a site’s real list of jobs before reading it.

  • the site’s links
How it works

Before a site you can only open yourself is read, Jobadabra looks for its job list: the site's own Careers link first, then Claude's pick among its links, then a "<company> jobs" web search, preferring your country's page. The address is kept for the site; one that shows no jobs twice is dropped and looked for again.

Gets past a dead end

Picks up to two steps toward the list: a “See all jobs” button, a country picker.

  • first lines of the page, your search words
  • your CV; it never signs in, applies or pays
How it works

When a job site you opened shows no jobs even after Claude's reading (a "See all jobs" button, a country picker, a search box), Claude picks at most two steps toward the list among the page's own buttons, links and search boxes, from its first lines of text and your search words, never your CV. It never signs in, applies, saves, pays or answers a robot check: when the page needs you for that, it says so and stops. When the fast model sees no way on, a stronger one looks once more. Cookie banners are closed with the least consent offered.

Filters for your search

On LinkedIn, Indeed or an employer’s site, picks the page’s filters that fit you.

  • filter names, your search words
  • your CV, the page
How it works

On a job list you opened yourself (LinkedIn, Indeed, an employer's site), the extension's "Filter for my search, then read" asks Claude which of that page's own filters fit your search: your places, your kind of role, recent jobs. Claude sees only the filters' names and your search words, never your CV or the page. It never touches Apply, Sign in, Save or Message; then the jobs on the page are read and scored like any other.

Why so few jobs

Says what would bring more, easiest first; AI explains only on your click.

  • the numbers of the search
  • your CV
How it works

When a search brings few new jobs, buttons say what would bring more, easiest first: a role word you miss, a filter of yours that hides jobs, a nearby place, a job source to connect. "Explain with AI" asks Claude, on your click only, why and what to do first, from the numbers of the search (never your CV).

Facts quoted from the posting

Languages, seniority, salary and visa, with the words they came from.

  • the posting’s description
How it works

Languages, seniority, salary and visa read from each description by AI, with the words they came from.

Hints from what people dismiss

Why jobs get turned down nudges the score, across installs.

  • counts only
  • a job or a name
How it works

Why jobs get turned down (seniority, place, tech) is counted across installs and nudges the score. Counts only, never a job or a name.

A finding a day

One useful discovery about your search, with evidence and one action.

  • your search results
How it works

One useful discovery about your search each day, with its evidence and one action. A weekly report on Mondays.

Your details from your CV

Fills in phone, street, town and more from your CV; you check and Save.

  • your CV, your Profile
  • nothing is kept until you Save
How it works

The details forms ask for and you haven't given yet (phone, street, postal code, town, date of birth, salutation) are read from your CV by Claude (and from your Profile, for what only it says, such as your town), once per CV and Profile, and put in the empty boxes of Profile → Your details, each marked "From your CV". Nothing is kept until you check them and press Save; a detail you typed is never replaced. Claude never guesses: a salutation is proposed only when your CV shows it, and it and anything taken from your Profile are marked "check it".

Strategy from your CV

Goals, a draft Profile and search settings in one pass; you review first.

  • your CV
How it works

Goals, a draft Profile, standard answers and search settings from your CV in one pass. You review before anything is saved. A rebuild keeps your places and work rights, and never rules jobs out over a language your CV doesn't mention.

Strategy insights

What your best-fit jobs ask for and where your target pay stands.

  • your jobs and Profile
How it works

What your best-fit jobs ask for, how much of the market your keywords catch, and where your target pay stands.

Reordered and reworded toward the posting, from your own facts only.

  • invented facts
How it works

Bullets reordered and reworded toward the posting, using only facts from your CV. Numbers are checked by code, unknown tools are flagged, and the page keeps the look of your own CV.

  • your CV
How it works

Every edit marked and explained next to a clean final PDF. You see what moved and why before you send it.

Cover letter and answers

A letter and an answer per question, in the posting’s language.

  • your Profile, the posting
How it works

A cover letter and an answer for each form question, drafted per job from your Profile, in the language the posting is written in. It also checks visa and language eligibility.

Knows more of your experience

Reads your other CVs or LinkedIn export and adds only the details your main CV lacks.

  • your extra CVs, LinkedIn export
  • it never changes your jobs, titles or dates
How it works

When you add another version of your CV, or your LinkedIn data export, Claude reads it and compares it with your main CV: which job each one describes, and which details the main CV does not state yet. Only those details are offered to tailoring, inside the jobs your main CV already has; its jobs, titles and dates never change, and a detail you did not write is never invented.

A check of your own CV

How cleanly a hiring system can read it, then a review of keywords and clarity.

  • your CV
How it works

How cleanly a hiring system can read it (free rules on the PDF), then an AI review of keywords, evidence and clarity. Not a score from a real hiring system: they publish none.

CV against this posting

Which asked-for terms your CV states, implies or never mentions.

  • your CV, the posting
How it works

Which terms the posting asks for your CV states, only implies or never mentions, and which requirements could be yes/no questions on the form. A grade that is a sort order, not a verdict.

Fills the form

Fills every field from your kit; marks what Claude had to answer.

  • your kit, the form’s fields
How it works

The extension fills every field from your kit. Claude answers only what the kit does not, and marks those answers for a read-through.

Knockout questions first

Work permit, sponsorship, location: moved to the top, marked.

  • Plain rules, no AI
How it works

Work permit, sponsorship, location and office-day questions are marked and moved to the top, because an employer can set them to reject (rules, no AI).

Postings with no form

Finds the email, call or visit that says how to apply.

  • 12 short lines of the posting
  • what you typed, your data
How it works

When the page does not say clearly how to apply, the AI reads up to 12 short numbered lines of the posting (sentences, links, buttons, an address or a phone number; never what you typed, never your data) and names the one that says how to apply: an email, a call, a visit. It can only point at a line that is on the page; it never sends or presses anything for you.

Written questions, drafted

Drafts an open answer in your voice; you read it first.

  • your CV, saved answers
  • it invents nothing
How it works

An open question in your own words (an incident you root-caused, why this company) is drafted from your CV, experience bank, LinkedIn export and saved answers, in your voice; an outline to complete where they say too little. Nothing is invented, and it stays marked until you have read it. "Review my answer" tells you what is missing and offers one improved version, typed only when you press it.

Schools, years, current job

Answers School, Degree, year and Job title in any language.

  • your CV’s employers and schools
How it works

Once your CV is imported, the form AI reads its employers and schools as written, so a School, Degree, year, Current company or Job title is answered in any language. It never imports the CV in the middle of a fill.

A tip at every step

How it works

One slim line of advice on Jobs, Focus, Interviews, Application sessions and on the form itself. Each is labelled Fact, Recruiters say, Worth trying or Tip, so you see how strong it is. One click hides them all.

Learns from every form

A question you answer once is answered for good.

  • what you already gave
How it works

Each form that leaves fields teaches reusable notes from what you already gave. A question you answer once is answered for good, and one your saved answers already settle (a work permit settles “need sponsorship?”) comes with its answer to accept. A new question waits with its job until a second employer asks it, then it is kept with the answer you gave. Each answer goes to its one place: Claude tells a fact about you (your right to work, notice period, the salary you ask for, a language) from a standard answer, and the fact goes to Profile → Your details.

TRACK

Reads your inbox so you do not have to

Replies, interviews and rejections, sorted onto the right job.

Understands any language

Interview or rejection? It decides from a fixed list, in any language.

  • one item at a time
  • your mail, in the shared list
How it works

Whether a calendar event is an interview, a reply asks you to book a time, a round was a recruiter screen or a technical one, which code and link in a sign-up email confirm your account: Claude decides from a fixed list of answers, in any language, once per item, instead of English keyword lists. Without AI, the answers you got before still come, from a shared list of meanings that starts with the old rules and grows with every language an install learns (only public wording: places, titles, labels; never your mail).

Gmail and Calendar, read-only

Each email matched to the right application.

  • sender, subject, first line
  • unrelated mail in full
How it works

Each email sorted into replies, interviews, rejections or recruiter pitches, and matched to the right application. Any language: no keyword lists. Mail from job systems, LinkedIn, recruiters and the companies you track is always read; for every other new inbox email (not Promotions, Social or Forums), a small model sees only its sender, subject and first line and says whether it is about your job search. Only those are read in full, and each email is judged once.
